The 4 thread overlocker is the primary machine used to construct stretch knit garments. It joins fabric pieces together while finishing the raw edges in a single operation.

Correct overlocking produces strong, flexible seams that allow knit garments to stretch during wear without breaking.

Overlocking Seams

Good Overlocking Technique

Align fabric edges

Keep both fabric layers aligned as they enter the machine to maintain an even seam allowance.

 

UPLOAD IMAGE: Two fabric layers with cut edges aligned, entering the overlocker.

 

 

Let the machine feed

Do not pull or push the fabric. Allow the feed dogs to move the fabric naturally.

 

UPLOAD IMAGE: Hands resting lightly on the fabric, guiding without pulling, as the overlocker feeds.

 

 

Maintain a consistent seam allowance

Guide the fabric carefully to produce accurate seam widths throughout the garment.

 

UPLOAD IMAGE: Fabric edge running against the overlocker seam guide, showing an even seam allowance.

 

 

Support the garment

Support larger garment sections so their weight does not stretch the seam while sewing.

 

UPLOAD IMAGE: A large garment section supported on the machine table rather than hanging off the edge.

 

 

Inspect each seam

Check seam quality before continuing to the next construction step.

 

UPLOAD IMAGE: A completed overlocked seam being inspected, showing even stitching and both layers caught.

 

 

Quality Checks

Check each overlocked seam against the following before moving on:

  • Seams are straight and evenly sewn.
  • Both fabric layers have been caught in the stitching.
  • Thread tension is balanced.
  • No skipped stitches are visible.
  • No puckering or stretching has occurred.
  • Seam allowance is consistent.
  • Thread chain has been secured correctly where required.

 

Common Overlocking Faults

Common overlocking faults, causes and solutions

Fault

Possible cause

Solution
Uneven seam allowance Fabric not guided consistently. Use the seam guide and maintain a steady fabric position.
Fabric layers do not match Layers shifted while sewing. Align edges carefully before stitching and support the fabric.
Puckered seam Fabric stretched, or incorrect differential feed. Allow the machine to feed naturally and check the settings if required.
Raw edge visible Cutting width or seam allowance inconsistent. Guide the fabric consistently and inspect the seam width regularly.
Loose thread chain Thread chain not secured. Secure or finish the chain according to workplace procedures.
