Getting to Know the Lockstitch Machine

 

The lockstitch machine (also called a plain machine) is the most common industrial sewing machine used in the fashion industry.

 

Lockstitch Machine (301 Stitch)

Lockstitch machines are commonly used to join garment pieces together, sew straight seams and create visible topstitching.

The machine uses one needle, one bobbin and two threads to produce a strong, straight stitch.

Because the stitch looks the same on both sides of the fabric, it produces a neat, professional finish on woven fabrics.

Stitch description

The stitch is formed when the needle thread interlocks with the bobbin thread inside the fabric.

The threads lock together in the centre of the seam, creating a strong, balanced stitch that looks the same on both sides of the fabric.

 

Parts of the Lockstitch Machine

Before you can thread or operate an industrial lockstitch machine, you need to recognise the main parts of the machine. Throughout the semester your teacher will refer to these names during demonstrations and practical activities.

 

  1. Hole in thread post – holds the thread cone.
  2. Discs on thread post – guide the thread from the cone into the machine.
  3. Thread guide – directs the thread along the correct threading path.
  4. Tension disc – controls the tension of the upper thread.
  5. Take-up spring – maintains thread tension while stitching.
  6. Slack thread regulator – controls the amount of slack in the thread during sewing.
  7. Arm thread guide – guides the thread through the machine arm towards the take-up lever.
  8. Take-up lever – feeds the thread through the machine during stitch formation.
  9. Face plate thread guide – directs the thread down the front of the machine towards the needle.
  10. Thread guide (pigtail) – keeps the thread aligned before it reaches the needle.
  11. Needle bar thread guide – guides the thread into the eye of the needle.
  12. Eye of the needle – the final point the thread passes through before stitches are formed.
