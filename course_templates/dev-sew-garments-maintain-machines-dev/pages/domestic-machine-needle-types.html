Domestic Machine Needles

 

Choosing the correct machine needle makes a big difference to your sewing quality. The wrong needle can cause skipped stitches, broken thread, fabric damage or poor seam quality.

 

For this course: You will mostly use needles suitable for knit fabrics, especially ballpoint or stretch needles. Your teacher will guide you on the correct needle for each machine and fabric.

 

Common Needle Types

Sharp Needle

A sharp needle has a fine point designed to pierce woven fabrics.

Use for:

  • Standard woven fabrics
  • Microfibre
  • Taffeta
  • Coated fabrics

Watch for: Sharp needles can damage some knit fabrics because they pierce fibres rather than moving between them.

 

 

 

Ballpoint Needle

A ballpoint needle has a rounded tip. Instead of cutting through fabric fibres, it moves between them.

Use for:

  • Cotton knit
  • Tricot
  • Jersey knits
  • Swimwear fabrics

Best for this course: Knit garments such as track pants and hoodies.

Choosing the Correct Needle

Before sewing, think about:

  • Is the fabric woven or knitted?
  • Is the fabric lightweight, medium weight or heavy?
  • Does the fabric stretch?
  • Are you sewing through multiple layers?
  • Are you using a specialist machine such as a coverstitch machine?

 

 

 

Common mistake: Using a sharp needle on knit fabric can damage the fabric or cause skipped stitches. For knit fabrics, use a ballpoint or stretch needle unless your teacher instructs otherwise.
