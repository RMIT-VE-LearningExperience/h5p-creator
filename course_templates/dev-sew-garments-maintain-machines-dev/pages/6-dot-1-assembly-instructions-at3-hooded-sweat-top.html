6.1 Assembly Instructions – AT3: Hooded Sweat Top

 

 

 

 

 

The Hooded Sweat Top is your second complete knit garment in this cluster. It builds on the machine skills you developed making the Track Pant, and introduces new operations including fusing, buttonholes, a hood, set-in sleeves and a coverstitched hem.

The garment is made from knit cotton or cotton polyester and is constructed using the four thread overlocker, the lockstitch machine, the buttonhole machine and the coverstitch machine.

 

Assembly Plan

This is a set of instructions that machinists will use to make a product in the most efficient manner. It is broken up into five categories, often based on skill level. In a factory, a machinist will be responsible for various tasks, which are assigned based on their experience.

 

Observational Safety Checklist

Think about a hazard that you may have encountered during the construction of this product and conduct a risk assessment:

  • What is the hazard?
  • What could happen as a result of this hazard? and
  • What can you do to reduce/prevent this risk from occurring?

You will need to identify a different hazard for each product you make this semester, so be sure to vary your answers.

 

Safety Warning

This garment uses the buttonhole machine and the fusing press. Safety glasses are required when using the buttonhole machine, and the fusing press has hot surfaces. Only operate either machine after your teacher has demonstrated the safe operating procedure.

 

Quality Checklist

You will be asked to critique your finished product using a checklist to ensure that it meets industry standards. Check your work against the following as you sew, not only at the end:

  • Stitch quality – stitches are even, secure and suitable for stretch fabric.
  • Seam allowance – seams are sewn accurately and consistently.
  • Notches – pieces are matched correctly before sewing, particularly at the shoulder, armhole and hood.
  • Buttonholes – positioned accurately, cut cleanly and secure.
  • Threads – loose threads are trimmed as you work.
  • Pressing – seams are pressed where instructed before moving to the next stage.

Be sure to include your successes and challenges, and how you achieved this success or what you would do to overcome these challenges if you were to make this product again.

 

Tools and Equipment

Be sure to bring the following to class so that you can complete the construction of this product:

  • All of your sewing equipment
  • Cut out paper patterns using the blocks provided in class
  • Knit cotton or cotton polyester fabric for the body, sleeves, hood and facing
  • Rib fabric for the cuffs
  • Fusible interfacing for the front facing and buttonhole positions
  • Drawstring cord
  • RMIT label and care label
  • A safety pin (to guide the drawstring through the hood casing)

Note: the tools and equipment list is drafted from the pieces list in the assembly plan – please confirm the fusible type and drawstring specification with the teaching team, and whether fabric can be provided for students who do not bring their own.

 

 

Step-by-step Construction Guide

This guide provides fashion students and designers with the knowledge of the techniques and components essential to the assembly of sewn products, developing your understanding of which stitch and seam types to select for fabrics and garments, as well as the knowledge to construct a range of basic techniques to assemble entire garments using the correct components.

A good assembly process plan can increase the efficiency and quality, and decrease the cost and time, of the whole product manufacturing process – which is important, as product and production demand changes rapidly in today’s market, and saving time is saving money.

The assembly plan is divided into five sections: Preparation, Small parts, Sub-assembly, Assembly, and Finishing.

Additionally, there are four important key details:

  • Machine type – which machine is used (the bottom of the assembly plan page has the abbreviations)
  • Operation number – refers to the process in the operation detail
  • ISO Number – refers to the industry standard
  • Time – refers to the timing each operation takes

 

Preparation

 

Check that you have all the required pieces:

  1. RMIT label, care label
  2. Front facing x 1pr
  3. Front facing fuse x 1pr
  4. Front facing buttonhole fuse x 1pr
  5. Hood x 1pr
  6. Rib cuffs x 1pr
  7. Sleeves x 1pr
  8. Front body
  9. Back body

 

 

 

Steps 1.1–1.4 – Let's begin

Check your stitch length on lockstitch – 3.5 is ideal for this fabric.

1.1 Change 4 Thread overlocker needle size to size 16, M/S.

1.2 Inspect cut work and identify faults, re cut, if necessary, E.

1.3 Fuse hoodie facings.

1.4 Fuse buttonhole position placement on hood facing pieces using facing fuse. Mark buttonhole position on outer hood using the pattern template.

 

 

 

Small Parts

Steps 2.1–2.2 – Labels and drawstring

2.1 Stitch labels together, P/L.

2.2 Cut drawstring to 110cm, Hand.

 

 

 

Sub Assembly

Steps 3.1–3.3 – Cuffs, facing and hood

3.1 Join cuffs x 2, M/S.

3.2 Join facing at centre back (top) and centre front (chin). Overlock edges of facing (4 Thread overlocker), M/S.

3.3 Join hood at centre back and centre front, M/S.

 

 

 

 

Step 3.4 – Attach and bag out facing to hood

3.4 Attach and bag out facing to hood. Press seam allowances toward facing and flatstitch, M/S.

 

 

 

 

Steps 3.5–3.7 – Buttonholes, labels and facing

3.5 Sew buttonholes on hood at marked position. Cut buttonholes, M/S, B/H.

3.6 Attach labels at centre back, P/L.

3.7 Stitch down facing following the overlocked edges. Press, P/L, P.

 

 

 

 

 

 

 

Assembly

Steps 4.1–4.2 – Shoulders and sleeves

4.1 Attach front and back shoulders, M/S.

4.2 Place sleeves around armholes and stitch, matching notches at shoulder to shoulder seams, M/S.

 

 

 

NOTE: The assembly plan cites “See Video Step 10” for step 4.2 (attaching sleeves). Video Step 10 is missing from the AT3 Resources page – the list jumps from Step 09 to Step 11. It needs to be sourced and embedded here.

 

Step 4.3 – Side seams

4.3 Join and stitch side seams from body hem to sleeve hem. Start at wearer's right sleeve hem to body hem. On wearer's left, start at body hem and stitch to sleeve hem, M/S.

Match at armhole seams.

 

 

 

 

Step 4.4 – Cuffs

4.4 Mark notches on cuffs and match cuffs to sleeve hems x 2, M/S.

 

 

 

Step 4.5 – Attach the hood

4.5 Attach hood to neckline matching notches (insert labels at centre back), M/S.

 

 

 

 

Finishing

Steps 5.1–5.2 – Hem and drawstring

5.1 Press hem 2.5cm – Coverstitch, E.

5.2 Knot drawstring ends and insert drawstring through hood casing using a safety pin to guide, E.

 

 

 

 

 

Steps 5.3–5.7 – Final checks and finishing

5.3 Trim all threads, E.

5.4 Final Inspection, E.

5.5 Final press, P.

5.6 Attach swing ticket, TKT.

5.7 Package, PK.
