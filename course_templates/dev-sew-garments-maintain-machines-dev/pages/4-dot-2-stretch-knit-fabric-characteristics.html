Stretch Knit Fabric Characteristics

 

Image generated by RMIT using Adobe Express

Stretch knit fabrics are commonly used in apparel because they are comfortable, flexible and able to move with the body.

Before cutting or sewing stretch fabric, it is important to understand how the fabric behaves. This will help you select the correct sewing method, control the fabric during construction and achieve a quality finish.

Working with Stretch Knit Fabric

Key Characteristics of Stretch Knit Fabrics

Characteristics of stretch knit fabrics

Characteristic

Description
Wrinkle resistant Knit fabrics naturally resist wrinkles. Even if scrunched or folded, they generally return to their original shape with minimal creasing, making them ideal for everyday wear and travel.
Elastic nature Because knit fabrics are made from one continuous yarn forming loops, they naturally stretch. Most stretch more across the width than along the length. Excessive stretching may permanently distort the fabric or cause runs.
Lightweight Many knit fabrics are lightweight, making them comfortable for everyday clothing. Heavier knit fabrics can also be produced by using thicker yarns or specialised knit structures.
Comfortable Knit fabrics are soft, flexible and comfortable to wear. Their stretch allows freedom of movement and provides a comfortable fit across a wide range of body shapes.
Faster to produce Knit fabrics are generally quicker to manufacture than woven fabrics because fewer production steps are required. This contributes to efficient garment production.
Cost effective The simpler manufacturing process often makes knit fabrics less expensive to produce than woven fabrics, helping reduce production costs.
Stretch and flexibility Knit fabrics stretch easily and conform to the body’s shape. Their flexibility allows many garments to be worn without closures such as zippers while still maintaining comfort and fit.
Softness Most knit fabrics have a soft handle. Depending on the knit structure they may feel smooth, textured or ribbed, but they are generally comfortable against the skin.

 

Before Sewing Stretch Knit Fabric

Before sewing, examine your fabric and identify the following:

  • Inspect the cut pieces for visible faults, damage or incorrect cutting before beginning construction.
  • Which direction has the greatest stretch?
  • Does the fabric recover well after stretching?
  • Does the fabric curl at the cut edge?
  • Is the fabric stable, slippery, lightweight or difficult to control?
  • Does the fabric have a clear right and wrong side?
  • Is the fabric suitable for the garment style and construction method?

 

Best Practice When Handling Stretch Fabrics

Handling techniques for stretch fabrics and why they matter

Technique

Why it matters
Support the fabric Keep the fabric supported on the sewing table before and after the needle. Allowing fabric to hang can stretch it out of shape before it is stitched.
Allow fabric to relax Avoid stretching or hanging knit fabric before sewing. Keeping the fabric flat helps maintain its original shape and size.
Do not pull Let the machine feed the fabric naturally. Pulling the fabric while sewing may create stretched or wavy seams.
Keep layers aligned Use pins or clips where appropriate to keep fabric layers together and prevent one layer feeding faster than the other.
Sew at a consistent speed A steady sewing speed gives the machine time to feed the fabric evenly and helps maintain stitch quality.
Test first Always test machine settings and stitches on a fabric offcut before beginning your garment.

 

Common Problems When Sewing Stretch Fabrics

Common problems, causes and solutions

Problem

Possible cause

Solution
Wavy seams Fabric stretched while sewing. Support the fabric and avoid pulling it through the machine.
Uneven seam lengths Fabric layers shifted during sewing. Pin or clip layers together and sew steadily.
Twisted seams Fabric was not aligned before stitching. Match notches and keep edges aligned throughout sewing.
Misshapen garment Fabric distorted during handling. Handle pieces gently and avoid excessive stretching.

 

Selecting Thread for Knit Garments

Thread selection is important when sewing stretch knit garments. The thread must be strong enough to withstand stretching, movement and washing without breaking or causing seam failure.

Thread types for knit garments

Thread type

Suitable?

Reason
Polyester thread Recommended Strong, durable and able to withstand the stretch required in standard knit garments.
Cotton thread Not recommended Has less stretch and may break when knit seams are placed under stretch pressure.
Specialist stretch thread Specialist use Used in high performance activewear where extra stretch and recovery are required.

 

Remember

Polyester thread is suitable for the standard knit garments made in this course because it has slight natural elasticity, good durability and excellent colour fastness.

Key Insights

Fabric Behaviour

Careful Handling

Supporting fabric, avoiding pulling and sewing steadily helps prevent distortion and sewing faults 

 

Correct Materials

Appropriate thread and sewing methods improve seam durability and garment performance
