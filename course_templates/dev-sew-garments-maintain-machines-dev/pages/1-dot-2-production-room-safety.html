Production Room Safety

Working safely is an essential part of the fashion industry. Before using any industrial sewing machine, you must understand the production room rules, wear the correct PPE and know how to set up your workstation safely.

 

Production Room Rules

Refresh your knowledge by reviewing the Production Room Rules page before class. This will help you remember the safety requirements, PPE expectations and production room procedures you will be using throughout the semester.

 

Personal Protective Equipment (PPE)

  • Closed-toe shoes are required whenever you are in the production room.
  • Long hair must be tied back before operating machinery.
  • Safety glasses are required when using selected specialist machines, including buttonhole machines.
  • Metal mesh gloves are only used when operating specialist cutting equipment under teacher supervision.
  • Loose jewellery should be removed so it cannot become caught in moving machinery.

 

 

 

Setting Up Your Workstation

  1. Store bags under the cutting tables or in the designated storage area.
  2. Make sure your table and floor area are clear.
  3. Check that the foot pedal area is free from obstructions.
  4. Make sure the machine is threaded correctly.
  5. Remove loose threads, pins and fabric scraps from the machine.
  6. Sew a test sample on scrap fabric before beginning your garment.

 

 

 

Hazard, Risk and Control

Hazards, risks and risk controls in the production room

Hazard

Risk

Risk control
Long hair May become caught in equipment. Tie long hair back before operating machinery.
Loose clothing or accessories May become caught in moving machinery. Wear close-fitting, comfortable clothing and remove loose accessories.
Exposed feet Sharp or heavy objects may fall onto feet, causing injury. Wear fully enclosed shoes suitable for workshop environments.
High heels Reduced stability may increase the risk of slips, trips or ankle injuries. Wear flat, sturdy footwear with good grip.
Floating fibres and dust Can irritate airways and contribute to breathing difficulties. Wear a dust mask where required and keep work areas clean.
Loud noise May cause distraction or stress, and can contribute to hearing damage over time. Wear hearing protection where required and minimise unnecessary noise.
Cluttered walkways and work areas Can result in trips, slips and falls. Keep walkways clear and store equipment in designated locations.
Poor lighting Can lead to eye strain and increase the chance of mistakes or accidents. Make sure adequate lighting is available before beginning work.
Extreme temperatures May result in heat stress, burns or dehydration. Work in suitable environmental conditions and take breaks as required.
