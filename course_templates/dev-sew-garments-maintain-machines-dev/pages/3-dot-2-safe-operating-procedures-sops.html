Safe Operating Procedures (SOPs)

 

Image generated by RMIT using Adobe Express

A Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) is a written set of instructions that explains the safest and most consistent way to complete a task.

In the production room, SOPs help you use machines safely, reduce faults and produce work that meets quality standards.

 

Safety Warning

If a machine sounds wrong, feels wrong or is not sewing correctly, stop sewing and ask your teacher before continuing.

 

Why Are SOPs Important?

The purpose of SOPs and why each one matters

SOPs help you to…

Why it matters
Work safely Reduces the risk of injury and helps control hazards.
Produce consistent quality Helps make sure garments and samples are made the same way each time.
Reduce mistakes Prevents avoidable faults, rework and wasted materials.
Protect equipment Supports correct machine use, cleaning and maintenance.
Train new staff Gives clear instructions so everyone follows the same process.

 

Following an SOP in the Production Room

Before You Start

  • Wear required PPE.
  • Prepare your workstation.
  • Check the machine is clean.
  • Check the correct needle and thread are fitted.
  • Check oil levels where required.
  • Sew a test stitch on scrap fabric.

 

While Sewing

  • Follow the operating procedure.
  • Keep hands clear of moving parts.
  • Guide the fabric, do not pull it.
  • Check stitch quality regularly.
  • Stop if something does not look, sound or feel right.
  • Ask your teacher if you are unsure.

 

After Sewing

  • Turn the machine off.
  • Remove fabric scraps and loose thread.
  • Clean the machine area if directed.
  • Return tools to the correct place.
  • Report faults to your teacher.
  • Leave the workstation clean for the next user.

 

 

If Something Goes Wrong

What to do when a problem occurs

If you notice…

You should…
Thread breaking Stop and check the threading, needle condition and tension.
Skipped stitches Check the needle type, needle condition and threading.
Machine sounds unusual Stop sewing and tell your teacher.
Fabric jams Turn the machine off before attempting to remove the fabric.
Needle breaks Turn the machine off and ask your teacher for assistance.
Machine fault Report it immediately. Do not continue sewing on a faulty machine.
