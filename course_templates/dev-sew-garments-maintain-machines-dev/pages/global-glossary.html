Glossary: Fashion Production Terms

This glossary contains common terms used throughout both Canvas shells. Use it whenever you come across a word or phrase you don't understand.

Assembly Plan

A document showing the order in which a garment is constructed.

Australian Standards

Rules and guidelines that help keep products, workplaces and processes safe and reliable.

Ball Point Needle

A sewing machine needle with a rounded tip designed for knit fabrics. It separates yarns instead of cutting them.

Breathability

A fabric's ability to allow air to pass through it, helping keep the wearer comfortable.

Construction

The method used to create a fabric or garment.

Courses

Horizontal rows of loops in a knitted fabric.

Coverstitch Machine

An industrial machine used to create professional hems on knit garments.

Differential Feed

An overlocker adjustment used to prevent stretching or create gathers while sewing.

Drape

The way a fabric hangs or falls when worn or displayed.

Edgestitch

A stitch sewn close to an edge or seam, usually 2–3 mm away.

Fabric Back (Wrong Side Up – WSU)

The inside surface of a fabric. In knitted fabrics, the back often has a horizontal zig-zag appearance.

Fabric Characteristics

The features or properties of a fabric, such as stretch, softness, durability, weight or breathability.

Fabric Face (Right Side Up – RSU)

The outside surface of a fabric. In knitted fabrics, the face usually shows visible "V" shaped stitches.

Fabric Structure

The way a fabric is constructed, such as woven, knitted or non-woven.

Feed Dogs

The metal teeth beneath the presser foot that move fabric through the machine.

Flatstitch

A stitch used to keep seam allowances flat and prevent facings from rolling to the outside.

French Terry Knit

A weft knitted fabric with a smooth outer surface and looped yarns on the inside. Commonly used for hoodies and sweatshirts.

Industrial Sewing Machine

A sewing machine designed for commercial production environments and extended use.

Jersey Knit

A knit fabric with a smooth face and looped reverse side. Commonly used for t-shirts.

Knitted Fabric

A fabric made from one or more yarns that are looped together.

Lockstitch Machine

The most common industrial sewing machine, used for straight stitching.

Machine Trouble Checklist

A document used to identify sewing machine problems and possible solutions.

Material Safety Data Sheet (MSDS)

A document containing safety information about hazardous substances.

Non-Woven Interfacing

A stabilising material used to add structure and support to specific areas of a garment.

Overedge Stitch

Another name for an overlock stitch.

Overlocker

An industrial machine that trims, sews and finishes fabric edges at the same time.

Pattern Notches

Small marks cut into pattern pieces to help align garment sections during sewing.

Plain Jersey Knit

A weft knit fabric with a smooth face and a reverse side showing horizontal loops.

Presser Foot

The machine component that holds fabric against the feed dogs while sewing.

Product Specifications

A document describing the requirements, measurements and construction details of a product or machine.

Quality Assurance (QA)

The processes put in place before production to help ensure quality standards are achieved.

Quality Control (QC)

The process of inspecting products during and after production to ensure quality standards are met.

Rib Knit

A knitted fabric with a raised vertical rib pattern. Commonly used for cuffs, waistbands and neckbands.

Ribs (Wales)

The vertical columns of loops visible in knitted fabrics.

Seam Allowance

The distance between the cut edge of the fabric and the stitching line.

Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs)

Written instructions explaining how a task should be completed safely and consistently.

Stitch Length

The length of individual stitches formed by a sewing machine.

Stitches per Centimetre (SPC)

The number of stitches formed within one centimetre of sewing.

Stitches per Inch (SPI)

The number of stitches formed within one inch of sewing.

Tension

The amount of pull applied to sewing thread during stitching.

Warp Knit

A knitted fabric made using multiple yarns running vertically through the fabric structure.

Weft Knit

A knitted fabric created using a single yarn moving horizontally across the fabric.

Welt Stitch

A decorative stitch sewn approximately 6 mm from an edge or seam.

Woven Fabric

A fabric created by interlacing two sets of yarns (warp and weft) at right angles to each other.
