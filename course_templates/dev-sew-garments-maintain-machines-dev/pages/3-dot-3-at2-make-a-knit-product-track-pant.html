AT2: Make a Knit Product – Track Pant

 

 

 

 

 

The Track Pant is your first complete knit garment in this cluster. You will use the machine skills developed in Weeks 1–3 to construct the garment safely, accurately and to an acceptable quality standard.

The pant is made from knit cotton or cotton polyester and is constructed using the four thread overlocker, the lockstitch machine and the coverstitch machine.

 

Assembly Plan

This is a set of instructions that machinists will use to make a product in the most efficient manner. It is broken up into five categories, often based on skill level. In a factory, a machinist will be responsible for various tasks, which are assigned based on their experience.

 

Observational Safety Checklist

Think about a hazard that you may have encountered during the construction of this product and conduct a risk assessment:

  • What is the hazard?
  • What could happen as a result of this hazard? and
  • What can you do to reduce/prevent this risk from occurring?

You will need to identify a different hazard for each product you make this semester, so be sure to vary your answers.

 

Quality Checklist

You will be asked to critique your finished product using a checklist to ensure that it meets industry standards. Check your work against the following as you sew, not only at the end:

  • Stitch quality – stitches are even, secure and suitable for stretch fabric.
  • Seam allowance – seams are sewn accurately and consistently.
  • Stretch – seams stretch without breaking.
  • Notches – pieces are matched correctly before sewing.
  • Threads – loose threads are trimmed as you work.
  • Pressing – seams are pressed where instructed before moving to the next stage.

Be sure to include your successes and challenges, and how you achieved this success or what you would do to overcome these challenges if you were to make this product again.

Step-by-step Construction Guide

This guide provides fashion students and designers with the knowledge of the techniques and components essential to the assembly of sewn products, developing your understanding of which stitch and seam types to select for fabrics and garments, as well as the knowledge to construct a range of basic techniques to assemble entire garments using the correct components.

A good assembly process plan can increase the efficiency and quality, and decrease the cost and time, of the whole product manufacturing process – which is important, as product and production demand changes rapidly in today’s market, and saving time is saving money.

The assembly plan is divided into five sections: Preparation, Small parts, Sub-assembly, Assembly, and Finishing.

Additionally, there are four important key details:

  • Machine type – which machine is used (the bottom of the assembly plan page has the abbreviations)
  • Operation number – refers to the process in the operation detail
  • ISO Number – refers to the industry standard
  • Time – refers to the timing each operation takes

 

Preparation

 

Check that you have all the required pieces:

  1. RMIT label, care label
  2. Waistband
  3. Elastic
  4. Front x 1pr
  5. Back x 1pr
  6. Rib cuffs x 1pr

 

 

 

Steps 1.1–1.3 – Let's begin

Check your stitch length on lockstitch – 3.5 is ideal for this fabric.

1.1 Change 4 Thread overlocker needle size to size 16, M/S.

1.2 Inspect cut work and identify faults, re cut, if necessary, E.

1.3 Stitch labels together, PL/M.

1.3 Stitch waist elastic – overlap ends 2cm and stitch a rectangle box, PL/M.

 

 

 

Small Parts

Steps 2.1–2.2 – Cuffs and waistband

2.1 Join cuffs x 2, M/S.

2.2 Join Waistband, M/S.

 

 

 

Assembly

Steps 3.1–3.2 – Side seams

3.1 Join and stitch side seams of front to back leg x 2 – creating a pair of legs, M/S.

3.2 Press all seams towards the back, P.

 

 

 

 

 

 

Steps 4.1–4.2 – Crotch seam and waist elastic

4.1 Join front and back crotch seam (circle) – joining legs together, M/S.

4.2 Insert elastic to waistband, make sure to stay stitch elastic to centre back waistband. Fold waistband in half and stay stitch 1cm at side seams and centre front to prevent elastic rolling, PL/M.

 

 

 

 

 

Steps 4.3–4.4 – Waistband and cuffs

4.3 Attach waistband to pant waist (follow notches), insert labels at the same time, M/S.

4.4 Attach cuffs to leg hems – (follow notches), M/S.

 

 

 

 

NOTE TO LD TEAM: The assembly plan refers to “See Video Step 12 & Video Step 13” for step 4.4 (attaching the cuffs). Only 11 video links exist on the AT2 Resources page. Videos 12 and 13 need to be sourced and embedded here, or the assembly plan needs correcting.

 

Finishing

Steps 5.1–5.5 – Final checks and finishing

5.1 Trim all threads, E.

5.2 Final Inspection, E.

5.3 Final press, P.

5.4 Attach swing ticket, TKT.

5.5 Package, PK.
