Pressing During Garment Construction

 

Image by aerogondo on Adobe Stock Licenced under Education License 

Pressing is an important part of garment construction. Rather than waiting until the garment is finished, professional machinists press garments throughout production to help seams sit flat, improve accuracy and produce a high-quality finish.

  • Pressing improves garment appearance and accuracy.
  • Pressed seams are easier to align during construction.
  • Regular pressing reduces bulk in seam intersections.
  • Professional pressing contributes to the overall quality of the finished garment.

Pressing Your Garment

Pressing Throughout Construction

After joining seams

Press seams flat to remove puckering and help the seam sit correctly before continuing construction.

 

 

 

Before attaching new components

Flat garment sections are easier to align accurately when attaching waistbands, cuffs and other components.

Reducing bulk

Pressing helps flatten seam allowances and reduces bulk where multiple seams meet.

 

 

 

Final press

Complete a final press to improve garment presentation before the quality inspection and assessment submission.

 

 

 

Good Pressing Practices

Good pressing practices and why they matter

Practice

Why it matters
Press, do not iron Lift and lower the iron rather than sliding it across the fabric. This helps prevent stretching or distortion.
Use the correct temperature Different fabrics respond differently to heat. Always follow workplace procedures and test if required.
Press every major seam Frequent pressing improves construction accuracy and creates a neater finished garment.
Avoid over-pressing Excessive heat or pressure can flatten fabric texture, create shine or damage synthetic fibres.
Allow fabric to cool Cooling helps the fabric retain its new shape after pressing.

 

Common Pressing Problems

Common pressing problems, causes and prevention

Problem

Possible cause

Prevention
Fabric stretched out of shape Iron pushed across the fabric. Use an up-and-down pressing motion rather than sliding.
Shiny fabric surface Iron temperature too high. Use the correct heat setting and a press cloth where appropriate.
Seams remain bulky Garment not pressed after construction steps. Press seams after each major operation.
Wrinkles remain Insufficient steam or pressure. Apply appropriate steam and allow the fabric to cool before handling.
Fabric damaged Incorrect temperature or prolonged contact. Always check fibre content and workplace pressing procedures.
