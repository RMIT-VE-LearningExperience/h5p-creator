Quality Standards and Fault Identification 

 

Image generated by RMIT using Adobe Express

Quality checking is an important part of garment production. Every sewing sample and garment should be inspected while you sew, not only at the end.

Use the sections below to review quality standards, identify common sewing faults and understand what actions to take when a problem occurs.

 

What Does Quality Mean?

In the clothing industry, quality standards help make sure garments are safe, functional and professionally constructed. Quality stitching should meet the requirements of the garment, fabric type, machine type and specification.

Quality stitching should be:

  • Even and consistent
  • Free from loose threads
  • Secure and durable
  • Suitable for the fabric type
  • Free from puckering and distortion
  • Completed using the correct machine

 

Quality Standards and Faults

Quality Assurance (QA) vs Quality Control (QC)

Producing high-quality garments requires more than good sewing skills. Throughout the production process, manufacturers use Quality Assurance (QA) to prevent problems and Quality Control (QC) to identify faults before garments reach the customer.

Quality Assurance (QA)

Putting systems in place before production begins to help make sure garments are made correctly.

 

Quality Control (QC)

Checking garments during and after production to identify faults and make sure they meet workplace quality standards.

 

 

Industry Quality Standards

The quality standards below will help you evaluate your sewing samples and garments throughout this unit. Applying these checks regularly will help you identify faults early and produce work that meets industry expectations.

Industry quality standards and what to check

Area

What to check
Machine selection The correct machine has been used for the operation and fabric type.
Stitch type The stitch is suitable for the seam, fabric and garment function.
Stitch quality Stitches are even, secure and free from skipped stitches.
Seam allowance Seams are sewn accurately and consistently.
Thread finish Loose threads are trimmed and seams are secure.
Fabric appearance Fabric is not puckered, stretched, twisted or damaged.
Pressing Work has been pressed where required and lies flat.
Specification The sample or garment matches the instructions, assembly plan or teacher demonstration.

 

Quality Inspection Checklist

Before submitting any sewing sample or garment, check:

  • Are all seams complete?
  • Are stitches even?
  • Are seam allowances consistent?
  • Has the correct machine been used?
  • Are there any skipped stitches?
  • Are there any broken threads?
  • Has excess thread been trimmed?
  • Does the garment lie flat without twisting?
  • Has the item been pressed if required?

Before You Move On

After each sewing operation, pause and check your work before continuing to the next step.

  • Trim loose threads.
  • Check stitch quality.
  • Check seam allowance.
  • Press if required.
  • Inspect the garment or sample.
  • Correct any faults before continuing.

 

Common Sewing Faults

Learning to identify sewing faults early will help you produce higher quality garments and reduce wasted time and materials.

Common sewing faults and their possible causes

Fault

What it looks like

Possible cause
Skipped stitches Missing stitches along the seam. Bent needle, incorrect needle, damaged needle or incorrect threading.
Puckering Fabric gathers or wrinkles beside the seam. Tension too tight, incorrect needle or fabric being pulled while sewing.
Thread breakage Thread snaps during sewing. Incorrect threading, damaged needle, excessive tension or thread catching.
Uneven stitching Stitches vary in length or appearance. Incorrect threading, tension issues, inconsistent sewing speed or incorrect fabric handling.
Seam slippage Fabric pulls apart at the seam. Incorrect seam allowance, unsuitable stitch type or poor stitch tension.
Fabric damage Holes, runs or pulled fibres. Wrong needle type, damaged needle or incorrect machine setup.

 

Machine Troubleshooting Guide

Most sewing problems can be solved by checking the machine setup before continuing. Always stop sewing if you notice a fault, identify the possible cause, correct the problem and sew another test stitch before returning to your garment.

Remember

Never continue sewing if you notice a fault. Small problems usually become much bigger if they are ignored.

 

Machine problems, causes and corrective actions

Problem

Possible cause

Corrective action
Skipped stitches Bent needle, damaged needle, incorrect needle or incorrect threading. Replace the needle, rethread the machine and sew another test sample.
Thread keeps breaking Incorrect threading, damaged needle, excessive thread tension or a burr on the needle. Rethread the machine, replace the needle and check the thread tension.
Puckered fabric Tension too tight, unsuitable needle or pulling the fabric while sewing. Adjust the tension, use the correct needle and guide the fabric without stretching it.
Uneven stitches Incorrect threading, inconsistent sewing speed or tension problems. Check the threading path, adjust the tension and sew another test stitch.
Machine jams Thread tangled inside the machine or excessive lint build-up. Turn the machine off immediately and ask your teacher before removing the jam.
Fabric will not feed Feed dogs obstructed, presser foot not lowered or incorrect handling. Check the machine setup and avoid pushing or pulling the fabric through the machine.
Needle breaks Incorrect needle, pulling the fabric, bent needle or incorrect machine timing. Stop sewing immediately, remove the broken pieces safely and replace the needle.
Loose loops underneath the fabric Upper thread incorrectly threaded or incorrect tension. Raise the presser foot, rethread the machine and test again.

 

When Should You Stop Sewing?

If any of the following happen, stop sewing immediately and ask your teacher for assistance.

  • The machine jams or locks.
  • The needle breaks.
  • The machine makes unusual noises.
  • You notice smoke, excessive heat or an unusual smell.
  • The machine continues to skip stitches after you have replaced the needle and checked the threading.
  • The safety guards become loose or damaged.

 

Corrective Actions

When a fault is identified, it should be corrected before production continues.

Machine Related

  • Replace damaged needles.
  • Rethread the machine.
  • Adjust thread tension.
  • Clean lint and thread build-up.
  • Sew a new test stitch.
  • Report faults that cannot be safely corrected.

 

Construction Related

  • Unpick incorrect stitching.
  • Resew faulty seams.
  • Trim loose threads.
  • Check seam allowances.
  • Press where required.
  • Inspect the work before continuing.
