Before constructing garments from knit fabrics, it is important to revisit the basic knit structures introduced in Semester 1.

Knit fabrics are made from interconnecting loops of yarn. This looped structure gives knit fabrics their stretch, flexibility and comfort.

Understanding knit structures will help you identify fabric types, select suitable sewing methods and manage fabric behaviour during garment construction.

 

Knit Structures

What is a Knit Fabric?

Knitted fabrics are made from one or more long yarns that interconnect and loop together. This is similar to traditional hand knitting using knitting needles and a ball of yarn.

This looped structure gives knit fabrics their stretch and flexibility. Knit fabrics are commonly used for casual wear, activewear, underwear, knit tops, dresses, cuffs, waistbands and many other garments.

Knit fabrics are manufactured on large knitting machines, which allows fabric to be produced quickly and efficiently.

 

How Stretch is Created

All knit fabrics have some stretch, but the amount and direction of stretch can vary significantly.

The stretch of a knit fabric is influenced by:

  • The yarn used
  • The knit structure
  • The density of the stitches
  • Whether elastane or another stretch fibre has been included

Some knits stretch mainly across the width of the fabric. Others stretch across both the width and the length.

 

Two Methods of Knitting

There are two main methods of knitting: weft knitting and warp knitting. These methods describe the way the loops are formed in the fabric.

Weft knitting and warp knitting compared

Method

Description

Diagram
Weft knitting One continuous yarn moves horizontally across the fabric, forming loops back and forth. Weft knits are commonly used in apparel and usually have good stretch.
Warp knitting Multiple yarns form vertical loops and move diagonally from wale to wale. Warp knits are generally more stable and are produced on different machinery from weft knits.

 

Tubular and Flat Knit Fabrics

Knit fabrics may be produced as tubular fabric or flat fabric, depending on the knitting machine used.

Tubular and flat knit fabric compared

Fabric form

Description

Image
Tubular knit Tubular knit fabric is knitted as a tube on a circular knitting machine. This means the fabric does not have a traditional selvedge edge.
Flat knit Flat knit fabric is knitted on a wide straight knitting machine and produced in rolls. Some flat knits have perforated lengthwise edges similar to a selvedge.

 

Identifying a Knit from a Woven Structure

Complex stitches and special finishes can sometimes make it difficult to tell whether a fabric is knitted or woven.

A simple way to test the fabric structure is to gently pull a thread from one crosswise end of the fabric.

Testing whether a fabric is knitted or woven

Fabric type

What to look for

Image
Knit fabric If loops appear when a thread is pulled from the edge, the fabric is likely to be a knit.
Woven fabric If a fringe appears when a thread is pulled from the edge, the fabric is likely to be woven.

 

Basic Knit Terms

The following terms are used when describing knitted fabric structure. All knit variations are created by changing the arrangement of basic knit and purl stitches.

Basic knit terms and their meanings

Term

Meaning

Diagram
Knit stitch A basic loop structure where a loop is drawn through the front of the previous loop.
Purl stitch A basic loop structure where a loop is drawn through the back of the previous loop.
Wales Lengthwise columns of loops. These appear as vertical stitches in the fabric.
Courses Crosswise rows of loops. These appear as horizontal rows in the fabric. See the wales and courses diagram above.
Gauge The number of stitches per centimetre. A higher gauge usually produces a finer fabric.
