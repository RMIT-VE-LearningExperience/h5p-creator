Your Workstation

Your workstation includes sewing areas, cutting tables, patternmaking tables, fabric laying areas and pressing stations. These spaces are shared by all students and must be kept clean and tidy at all times.

Before, during and after class

  • Keep your table and working area clean and tidy.
  • Store bags and personal items under the large cutting tables.
  • Keep walkways clear of tripping hazards.
  • Dispose of thread, paper and fabric scraps in the correct bins.
  • Place broken needles in the designated yellow sharps bin only.
  • Leave shared work areas ready for the next person.

 

Key rule

A clean workstation is a safe workstation.

 

Production Room Equipment
 

Important

Only operate a machine if you have been shown how to use it safely and have permission from your teacher.

 

The production room contains a range of industrial machines used in the clothing and textile industry. Each machine has a specific purpose and specific safety requirements.

Equipment you may use includes

  • Lockstitch machines
  • 3 thread and 4 thread overlockers for woven and knit fabrics
  • Automatic buttonhole machines
  • Coverstitch machines
  • Binding machines
  • Fusing machines
  • Steam irons

General equipment safety rules

  • Take care near rotating or moving components.
  • Avoid contact with hot surfaces.
  • Wear personal protective equipment when required.
  • Take care near electrical connections.
  • Follow workplace instructions and Standard Operating Procedures.
  • Stop and ask for help if you are unsure.

Working Safely in the Environment

Lighting

Lighting must be adequate for detailed sewing, cutting and patternmaking work. The best environment uses a combination of natural and artificial lighting.

 

 

 

Floor area

Walkways and floor areas must be kept clear of fabric scraps, electrical cables, bags and other tripping hazards.

 

 

 

Ventilation

Fresh air is important in all work environments. Windows and ventilation systems help maintain a safer and more comfortable production room.

 

 

 

Fire safety equipment

Fire fighting equipment, including extinguishers and fire hose reels, must remain clearly marked, accessible and unobstructed at all times.

 

 

 

Remember

Know where emergency exits, fire extinguishers and fire hose reels are located.

 

Ergonomics

Ergonomics refers to setting up the work area to suit the user and reduce the risk of injury. Good posture and correct equipment setup are important when sewing, cutting, pressing and patternmaking.

Safe ergonomic practices

  • Use a stable cutting table at the correct height.
  • Sit correctly at sewing machines.
  • Adjust your chair where possible.
  • Keep your work close enough to avoid over-reaching.
  • Take care when completing repetitive tasks.
  • Report discomfort or equipment issues to your teacher.

Posture check

When seated at a machine, consider the type of chair you are using, how it is adjusted and how you sit while working.

 

Personal Protective Equipment

Personal Protective Equipment, or PPE, helps protect you from injury. Some PPE must be worn at all times, while other PPE is required only when using specific equipment.

Before working in the production room

  • Tie back long hair.
  • Secure or trim long beards.
  • Remove jewellery, including watches, necklaces, bracelets, earrings and rings.
  • Wear tight or semi-fitted clothing.
  • Avoid loose or baggy clothing that could become caught in equipment.
  • Wear enclosed, sturdy footwear at all times.

Food, drink and phones

  • Do not eat or drink in the cutting room.
  • Keep phones away while using machinery.
  • Take phone calls outside the production room.
  • Do not use earphones or headphones while working in the production room.
 

Footwear requirement

Enclosed, sturdy footwear must be worn at all times in the production room. High heels and open shoes are not suitable.

 

Emergency Procedures

If there is a fire or other emergency, it is important that you respond quickly and follow instructions.

In an emergency

  1. Stay calm.
  2. Follow teacher and warden instructions.
  3. Stop working and make equipment safe if directed.
  4. Leave the room by the nearest safe exit.
  5. Do not use lifts.
  6. Move to the designated assembly area.
  7. Remain with your class group until advised otherwise.

Emergency reminder

Your teacher will show you the evacuation route and assembly area for your classroom.

 

Hazard Prevention and Control Measures

A hazard is anything that could cause harm. You are expected to identify hazards, reduce risks where possible and report concerns to your teacher.

Hazards, risks and control measures in the production room

Hazard

Possible risk

Control measure
Long hair May become caught in equipment. Tie hair back.
Loose clothing or accessories Could become caught in equipment. Wear fitted, comfortable clothing. Avoid loose accessories.
Exposed feet Sharp or heavy objects could injure your feet. Wear enclosed, sturdy shoes.
High heels May affect stability and cause ankle injury. Wear low, sturdy heels or flat shoes.
Floating fibres May cause breathing difficulties. Wear a dust mask when required.
Loud noise May cause stress, discomfort or distraction. Reduce volume where possible or use hearing protection if directed.

Poor lighting May result in eyestrain. Use appropriate natural and artificial lighting.
Extreme temperatures May cause burns or heat stress. Plan work times, take breaks and use air-conditioning where available.
