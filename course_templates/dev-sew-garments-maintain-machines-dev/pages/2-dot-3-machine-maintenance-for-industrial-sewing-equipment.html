Safety Warning

Always switch the machine power off before cleaning, oiling, tilting, adjusting or threading. Do not carry out any of these tasks unless your teacher has shown you how to do them safely.

 

Maintenance Procedures

Identifying Your Machine

Before operating an industrial sewing machine, you should be able to identify the machine and locate key machine information.

Machine details may include:

  • Machine name
  • Manufacturer
  • Model number
  • Serial number
  • Operating manual location
  • Next scheduled service or maintenance date

Preparing Your Workstation

Before beginning any sewing task, prepare your workstation, inspect your machine and complete a test stitch to make sure the machine is operating correctly.

Steps for preparing your workstation

Step

Action

Image
1 Follow PPE requirements, including closed-toe shoes and tying back long hair.  
2 Clear the workstation, machine table and foot pedal area of obstacles.
3 Check that the machine is threaded correctly.  
4 Remove loose threads, lint and fabric scraps from the machine area.
5 Switch the machine on and sew a test sample on scrap fabric.
6 Adjust the tension if the stitch or loop does not meet quality standards. Turn left to loosen and right to tighten.
7 Replace the needle if the test sample shows signs of needle damage.  
8 Turn the power off and leave the workstation clean when you have finished.

 

Checking Oil Levels

Oil lubricates the moving parts of an industrial sewing machine. Where required, oil levels must be checked to make sure the machine can operate safely and correctly.

Before you start

Make sure the machine power is switched off. Do not tilt, clean, oil, adjust or thread an industrial machine unless your teacher has shown you how to do it safely.

 

Steps for checking oil levels on a lockstitch machine

Step

Action

Image
1 Place your left hand through the handle and hold the top part of the machine with your right hand. Hold firmly.
2 Push up gently and tilt the machine back to reveal the parts underneath.
3 Check that the oil level sits between the maximum and minimum markers.
4 Check the oil colour. The oil should be clear. If it looks brownish, it needs to be changed.
5 Check for dust near the bobbin cage and remove it with tweezers.
6 Keeping your hands in the same position, gently tilt the machine towards you and back to its original position.

 

Cleaning an Overlocker

Regular cleaning helps an overlocker operate efficiently and reduces the build-up of lint and thread that can interfere with stitch formation.

Before you start

Make sure the machine power is switched off.

 

Steps for cleaning a 4 thread overlocker

Step

Action

Image
1 Assemble your tools: a double-ended brush, tweezers, clippers, a fabric scrap for wiping and a small fabric scrap to test the stitch after cleaning.
2 Push the right cover towards the right and open it.
3 Use the front lever to open the left side cover and position it towards the back.
4 Make sure the needle is up by rolling the side wheel. The needle must be clear of the feed dogs.
5 Pull the top foot lever towards you, then move the foot arm towards the back.
6 Starting at the front, use tweezers to gently remove thick yarn residue. Pull on the loose chain to keep the internal threads taut. Use a small brush to collect finer yarn residue.
7 Use a fabric scrap to wipe the inside covers, then close the front cover.
8 Make sure the needle is up by rolling the side wheel. Use the top lever to return the foot to its sewing position.
9 Close the left side cover. Make sure it clicks into position.
10 Turn the power on and sew a test stitch using a clean fabric scrap.

Signs a Machine Needs Attention

Common machine problems and what to do

What you notice

Possible issue

What you should do
Machine sounds louder than normal Machine may require cleaning or servicing. Stop sewing and tell your teacher.
Thread continually breaks Incorrect threading, damaged needle or incorrect tension. Rethread the machine and check the needle.
Skipped stitches Incorrect or damaged needle. Replace the needle and sew another test stitch.
Fabric is not feeding correctly Feed problem or incorrect handling. Stop sewing and ask your teacher for assistance.
Excessive lint build-up Machine requires cleaning. Turn the machine off and clean it following the approved procedure.
Oil appears brown or dirty Oil may require replacement. Report the machine to your teacher. Do not change the oil yourself.

 

 

Tip

Professional machinists inspect and clean their machines throughout the day, not just at the end of a shift. Taking a few minutes to remove lint, check the needle and sew a test stitch can prevent faults before they affect garment quality.
