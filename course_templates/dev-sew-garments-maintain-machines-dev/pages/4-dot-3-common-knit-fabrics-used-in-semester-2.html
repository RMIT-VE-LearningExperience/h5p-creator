Common Knit Fabrics Used in Semester 2

 

Image generated by RMIT using Adobe Express

Throughout Semester 2 you will work with several different knit fabrics while completing machine samples and constructing garments.

Although all knit fabrics contain loops and have stretch, they differ in their appearance, stretch, recovery, weight and intended end use.

Understanding the characteristics of each fabric will help you select appropriate sewing techniques and produce high-quality garments.

 

The Three Knit Fabrics

Plain Jersey

Structure

  • A single jersey is a weft knitted fabric produced using one set of needles.
  • The face of the fabric displays knit stitches.
  • The reverse side displays purl stitches.

Characteristics

  • Smooth face with visible vertical wales.
  • Horizontal purl loops on the reverse side.
  • Good stretch, particularly across the width.
  • Tends to curl at the cut edges.
  • Can unravel if damaged.
  • Produced on circular, flat bed and whole garment knitting machines.

Typical uses

  • T-shirts
  • Fashion tops
  • Dresses
  • Leggings
  • Hosiery
  • Lightweight knitwear

 

 

 

 

Rib Knit

Structure

  • A weft knitted fabric with alternating knit and purl stitches.
  • Both sides of the fabric appear almost identical.

Characteristics

  • Distinct vertical ribs (wales).
  • Excellent crosswise stretch.
  • Strong recovery after stretching.
  • Available in different rib combinations such as 1×1 and 2×2.
  • Greater elasticity than plain jersey.
  • Produced on circular, flat bed and whole garment knitting machines.

Typical uses

  • Cuffs
  • Waistbands
  • Neckbands
  • Hosiery
  • Tank tops

 

 

 

French Terry

Structure

  • A weft knitted fabric commonly used in activewear and casual apparel.
  • Made with a smooth face and looped yarns on the reverse side.
  • Additional yarns are held by the loops in each course of the fabric.

Characteristics

  • Light to mid-weight knit fabric.
  • Smooth outer surface.
  • Soft looped texture on the inside.
  • Natural stretch and comfort.
  • Absorbent and suitable for moisture management.
  • Often softer and more flexible than traditional fleece.

Typical uses

  • Hoodies
  • Sweatshirts
  • Track pants
  • Shorts
  • Casual tops
  • Activewear

Handling notes

Typical uses
Plain jersey Smooth face with purl loops on the reverse side. Good stretch, usually more across the width than the length. May curl at cut edges and can distort if pulled while sewing. T-shirts, tops, dresses, leggings and lightweight garments.
Rib knit Visible vertical ribs on both sides of the fabric. High crosswise stretch with strong recovery. Can stretch out if over-handled. Useful where firm recovery is needed. Cuffs, waistbands, neckbands, tanks and fitted garments.
French terry Smooth outer face with soft loops on the inside. Moderate stretch with comfortable movement. Usually more stable than lightweight jersey, but the thickness may add bulk to seams. Hoodies, sweatshirts, track pants, shorts and casualwear.
