Industrial Sewing Machine Needles

 

Image by katyamaximenko on Adobe Stock Licenced under Education License

A sewing machine needle carries the thread through the fabric to form each stitch. Selecting the correct needle is essential for producing quality stitching and preventing damage to both the fabric and the machine.

Using the wrong needle can result in skipped stitches, broken thread, damaged fabric, poor stitch quality or visible holes in the fabric.

 

Parts of a Sewing Machine Needle

The needle is made up of several parts. Each part helps the machine carry the thread through the fabric and form a stitch correctly.

  • Shank – clamped by the sewing machine’s needle holder.
  • Shoulder – where the thick shank tapers down to the shaft.
  • Shaft – passes through the fabric carrying the thread.
  • Groove – allows the thread to sit close to the needle as it passes through the fabric.
  • Scarf – provides space for the hook or shuttle to pass close by.
  • Eye – carries the thread.
  • Point – penetrates the fabric by parting the threads or cutting a hole.

 

 

 

Needle Sizes

Your choice of needle depends on the fabric weight and the type of needle point required. As a general rule, the finer the fabric, the finer the needle.

Needle sizes and suitable fabrics

Needle size

Suitable fabrics
70/10 Lightweight fabrics
80/12 Light to medium fabrics
90/14 Medium weight fabrics
100/16 Heavy fabrics

 

Needle Types

Universal Needle

  • General sewing
  • Woven fabrics
  • Basic construction

 

Ballpoint Needle

  • Knit fabrics
  • Jersey fabrics
  • Reduces fabric damage

 

 

Stretch Needle

  • Highly elastic fabrics
  • Activewear
  • Reduces skipped stitches

 

Denim Needle

  • Heavy fabrics
  • Canvas
  • Denim

 

 

Which needles will we use?

In this subject, you will mainly use ballpoint needles and stretch needles. These needle types are designed for sewing stretch knit garments, including track pants, hoodies and other knit apparel.

 

Choosing the Wrong Needle

Using the wrong needle can damage the fabric, reduce stitch quality and create sewing problems. Always select the correct needle for the fabric you are sewing.

Incorrect needle choices and their results

Incorrect needle choice

Possible result
Universal needle used on knit fabric Fabric damage or small holes.
Bent or damaged needle Skipped stitches and poor stitch formation.
Blunt needle Poor stitch quality, thread breakage and fabric damage.
Needle too large Visible holes or damage to the fabric.
Needle too fine The needle may bend or break while sewing.

 

 

Tip

One of the first troubleshooting steps in industry is to check the needle. Replacing a bent, blunt or incorrect needle often solves sewing problems quickly and improves stitch quality.
